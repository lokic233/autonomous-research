### A
- Vote: **YELLOW if AMD reproduces a finite ceiling at matched mapping pressure / RED if it does not** — and on the live data so far, leaning RED.
- Strongest remaining attack: the AMD "no ceiling at 4M pages" is a non-comparison. NVIDIA failed at 520K × 2MB = ~1TB of VA / ~520K mapping descriptors. AMD reached 4M × 4KB = ~16GiB of VA / 4M descriptors — different axis, lower total mapping pressure on the descriptor side only by a factor of ~8, and the run hit a *safety cap*, not a driver failure. "Higher-cap re-run in flight" admits the cross-vendor claim is unsettled. Even granting vendor divergence, "NVIDIA driver 580.82.07 + CUDA 12.8 enforces a 520K per-context mapping ceiling that AMD ROCm does not" is a forensic note pinned to two driver versions on two SKUs — not OSDI/ATC scope. Reviewer will ask: does it survive a driver bump? A second NVIDIA arch (B200)? A second AMD arch?
- Flip to GREEN requires: (a) AMD probe pushed to actual failure with matched total mapping descriptors AND matched total VA, both axes, showing either a much-higher finite ceiling OR genuinely unbounded; (b) reproduction of the NVIDIA ceiling across ≥2 CUDA versions and ≥2 NVIDIA architectures (H100 + B200/L40S) at the same K; (c) a mechanism hypothesis (which driver structure is O(mappings) and why) that the AMD divergence corroborates.
- Venue I'd bet on: HotOS short / SYSTOR / ATC short. Not OSDI as a standalone.

### B
- Vote: **YELLOW**.
- Strongest remaining attack: "superlinear" is what naive attention complexity (O(n²) recompute) already predicts; vLLM 4K→32K is 8× context for ~4× penalty (sub-quadratic, actually), and SGLang's curve is essentially the same shape. A reviewer reads the headline as "you measured what the transformer paper predicts." The genuinely-new piece — *position-dependent* penalty model, not just length-dependent — is asserted in the sharpening but not quantified in the evidence brief. Without that curve fit + a closed-form model + a baseline showing prior work *underestimated* the exponent, this is a strong workshop measurement note, not a MLSys paper.
- Flip to GREEN requires: (a) the position-dependent model presented as an actual equation with R² across both engines and ≥3 model sizes; (b) a third independent engine (TensorRT-LLM or HF TGI) reproducing the same exponent — two engines is suggestive, three is a workload property; (c) demonstration that at least one published prefix-cache paper's accounting *missed* this penalty by >2× under realistic agentic injection patterns. Tie the EDMM oracle ceiling to it as the headroom argument, not the solution.
- Venue I'd bet on: MLSys (measurement track) or ISCA workshop. Borderline 30–40% accept as currently scoped.

### C
- Vote: **YELLOW** — leaning RED on the sharpening question.
- Strongest remaining attack: against the FlashInfer baseline (Lab 3b: 5–22% gap), the win-region for HW VMM CoW is approximately empty in production — anyone running serious inference already uses a paged kernel. The remaining "must use unmodified contiguous-VA attention kernel" condition describes research prototypes, abandoned codepaths, and exotic attention variants without paged implementations. The project's own writeup concedes "NOT a practical capacity/speedup win over strong software baseline." A negative-result paper needs a *broad* design-space sweep to earn the venue; the current evidence is one cost decomposition (178µs = ~50µs cuMemSetAccess + ~30µs cuMemUnmap + 13µs D2D + remainder), one APC baseline, one null retraction. That's a section, not a paper. Also: 700× slower fork latency vs APC is operationally damning regardless of capacity.
- Flip to GREEN requires: (a) sweep across ≥3 attention implementations (SDPA, FlashAttention, FlashInfer, vLLM PagedAttention) × ≥3 prefix lengths × ≥3 branching fan-outs, producing a published regime map with crisp boundaries; (b) at least ONE concrete production scenario where HW VMM CoW wins by >2× on a metric a practitioner cares about (not "would win if you couldn't modify the kernel"); (c) breakdown of the 178µs showing which µs are fundamental to the API design vs implementation, with a credible path to closing the 700× APC gap (or proof it can't be closed).
- Venue I'd bet on: USENIX ATC negative-result track or EuroSys. ~25% as currently scoped; 40–45% with the sweep above.

### FINAL
- Zero GREEN today.
- Conditional: **A** upgrades to YELLOW (not GREEN) *only* if AMD's properly-matched re-run still shows no ceiling AND the NVIDIA ceiling reproduces across ≥2 CUDA versions on ≥2 NVIDIA arches.
- The single experiment that would change my vote: for **B**, run the same injection-position sweep on a third independent engine (TensorRT-LLM) at 4K/8K/16K/32K with 3 injection positions per length, fit `penalty = f(injected_position, total_length)`, and show the closed-form exponent matches across all three engines within ±10%. That elevates B from "measured what theory predicts" to "workload model with predictive power across the production engine landscape" — that I would vote GREEN on at MLSys.

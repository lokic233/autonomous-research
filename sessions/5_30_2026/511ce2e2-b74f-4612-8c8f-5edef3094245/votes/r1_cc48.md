**T1 — kernel-transparent physical KV sharing**
- Vote: **YELLOW**
- Strongest reject: "vAttention already delivers contiguous-VA VMM KV with unmodified kernels; your only delta is refcount+remap-on-write, which is incremental engineering, not an architectural contribution."

**T2 — VMM mapping-ceiling K≈520K characterization**
- Vote: **YELLOW**

- One-line rationale: Lab 1 is genuinely strong (392 VMAs vs 67M sysctl = 0.0006%, proving the wall is driver-internal not Linux), but it's "section not paper" — one number from one driver.

## PART B — NEW thesis candidates

### NEW-T1: A unified copy-on-write fork primitive over *heterogeneous-granularity* agent execution state (KV pages + RNG + append-only tool logs + retrieval cache), where per-domain page granularity is the core design tension.
- Repo / evidence basis: forkedkv Metric 5b (real Qwen2.5-7B CoW decode, 448 CoW events, bit-identical) + Metric 5c (ToT rollback copies exactly 1 page); CoW cost ~178µs/page of which only 13µs (7%) is D2D copy; repo 3 names "cross-domain CoW policy (KV+RNG+tool-logs+retrieval, heterogeneous page granularity)" as the genuinely-new architectural angle.
- Contribution type: abstraction / systems mechanism
- Closest prior work + why outside it: **CXLfork (ASPLOS'25)** forks homogeneous memory over a CXL/CPU medium; **vAttention** is KV-only and read-only. This is GPU-HBM fork across domains with *different* natural granularities under one refcounted VMM policy — neither addresses heterogeneous granularity.
- Closest OSS competitor + why it does not invalidate: vLLM/SGLang software refcounting handles *only* KV blocks; it has no primitive to fork RNG state or tool-log state atomically with the KV, so it cannot give a consistent forked agent state.
- Anti-FlashInfer test: **YES** — FlashInfer is an attention kernel; forking RNG/tool-logs/retrieval is orthogonal to attention speed.
- No-Code test: The abstraction survives — that agent fork must reconcile domains whose granularity spans 2MB pages to single-byte RNG, and that a per-page 178µs remap cost makes byte-granular domains pathological unless batched. That design tension is the knowledge.
- Baseline-death test: CXLfork (if its CPU fork is "good enough" for agent state, GPU-resident fork loses its reason to exist).
- Highest-EV experiment: Build CoW for ≥2 *non-KV* domains (RNG + tool-log) on top of the existing KV CoW; hypothesis = heterogeneous granularity forces a packing policy because 178µs/page remap dominates for small domains. Cost: ~2 weeks. Min success: demonstrate a workload where naive per-domain forking is >2× slower than granularity-aware packing. Kills: the "it's just glue code" attack.
- 30-day test: **Borderline-YES** — KV CoW already exists and is real; the novel non-KV domains are entirely unbuilt, which is the whole risk.
- Venue: ASPLOS (if ≥2 domains built) | else Workshop
- YOUR vote: **YELLOW** (GREEN only after ≥2 heterogeneous domains are built; today the novel part is vapor).

### NEW-T2: Mid-prompt tool-call injection breaks prefix-cache hash chains with a *superlinear, cross-engine* recompute penalty — a workload model for agentic serving.
- Repo / evidence basis: edmm vLLM live Radix penalty B/A=**8.21×**; P1.3 scaling vLLM 1.38×(4K)→5.41×(32K), SGLang 1.61×→5.24×, superlinear in **both** engines; standalone 16K penalty 4.18×.
- Contribution type: workload model / characterization
- Closest prior work + why outside it: SGLang RadixAttention (NeurIPS'24) documents prefix-cache *benefit*; Continuum/LMCache *assume* the penalty exists. None quantify the superlinear penalty of *mid-prompt* injection as a measured cross-engine property.
- Closest OSS competitor + why it does not invalidate: vLLM/SGLang are the *measured systems*, not competitors; neither ships a characterization of their own injection-penalty scaling curve.
- Anti-FlashInfer test: **YES** — this is hash-chain invalidation and recompute volume, independent of kernel throughput.
- No-Code test: The curve survives — "agentic tool injection causes superlinear KV recompute, ~8× live at realistic context, confirmed on two independent engines." That's a citable workload fact.
- Baseline-death test: Hard to kill (it is measurement); the real threat is the "everyone knows cache invalidation is bad" dismissal — countered by the superlinearity + magnitude + cross-engine reproduction.
- Highest-EV experiment: Broaden to 3+ engines × realistic agent traces (multi-tool, variable injection position); hypothesis = penalty exponent depends on injection *position*, not just length. Cost: ~2 weeks (mostly harness, data exists). Min success: a position-vs-penalty model that predicts the 8.21× point. Kills: the "obvious/known" dismissal.
- 30-day test: **YES** — most data already exists; this is the salvageable half of EDMM with the oracle solution removed.
- Venue: MLSys (full risky) | MLSys/ASPLOS Workshop (solid)
- YOUR vote: **YELLOW** (workshop-GREEN; see Part C).

### NEW-T3: GPU VMM CoW *loses* to software KV refcounting on fork latency and capacity — a crossover characterization of when hardware forking is justified, dominated by the per-page remap wall.
- Repo / evidence basis: vs vLLM-APC software baseline, software is **~700× faster on fork latency** and **~6× larger capacity** at 32-block prefix; CoW cost 178µs/page with only 7% (13µs) being actual data copy → 93% is remap/metadata; B8 NULL RESULT (scratch-pool only 3% faster, "47% removable" RETRACTED).
- Contribution type: characterization (honest negative result)
- Closest prior work + why outside it: vAttention/ChunkAttention report VMM *wins*; nobody publishes the *regime map* showing VMM CoW is the wrong tool below long-prefix / unmodified-kernel-required regimes. A negative-result design-space map is outside all of them.
- Closest OSS competitor + why it does not invalidate: vLLM-APC is the baseline that *beats* this — which is exactly the contribution (documenting where hardware should not be used).
- Anti-FlashInfer test: **Partial / NO** — if FlashInfer were infinitely fast, the "unmodified contiguous-kernel" upside (the only HW win) shrinks toward zero, narrowing the regime to nearly nothing. This is a genuine weakness and must be stated.
- No-Code test: The crossover model survives — "HW VMM fork pays off only when [long prefix AND custom non-paged kernel required]; otherwise software refcount dominates by ~700× latency / 6× capacity."
- Baseline-death test: vLLM-APC software refcounting (it already wins the headline axes).
- Highest-EV experiment: Sweep prefix length × branch fanout × kernel type to find the exact crossover frontier where HW VMM CoW becomes net-positive. Cost: ~1.5 weeks (bench harness exists). Min success: a non-empty region where HW wins on an axis software cannot match. Kills: the "HW forking is pointless" verdict — OR confirms it (still publishable as negative result).
- 30-day test: **YES** — pure measurement on existing harness.
- Venue: ATC/EuroSys (negative-result friendly) | Workshop
- YOUR vote: **YELLOW** (risk: the anti-FlashInfer failure may collapse the win-region to empty, making it a pure negative result).

### NEW-T4: Bit-identical KV-fork rollback as a *context-integrity primitive* for cheap recovery from prompt-injection / contaminated agent context.
- Repo / evidence basis: forkedkv Metric 5c (ToT rollback fires CoW exactly once, siblings uncorrupted, **bit-identical**); Metric 5b 448 CoW events bit-identical; edmm's contaminated-prompt framing (the thing it tried to recover speculatively).
- Contribution type: security primitive
- Closest prior work + why outside it: No cited prior frames GPU KV-fork as an integrity/rollback mechanism; vAttention is read-only, CXLfork is CPU and has no integrity threat model.
- Closest OSS competitor + why it does not invalidate: vLLM/SGLang can evict/recompute but cannot cheaply *restore a verified-clean branch* bit-identically without recompute.
- Anti-FlashInfer test: **YES** — rollback/isolation is orthogonal to kernel speed.
- No-Code test: The claim "CoW gives O(1-page) bit-identical rollback of contaminated context" survives as a primitive description.
- Baseline-death test: software snapshot/recompute (if recompute is cheap enough, no one needs O(1-page) rollback) — and the EDMM **multi-tenant IPC deadlock** shows the isolation story is unvalidated.
- Highest-EV experiment: Define a threat model (injected tool output) + measure rollback cost vs recompute under it. Cost: ~2 weeks incl. threat-model design. Min success: rollback ≥10× cheaper than recompute on a realistic injection trace. Kills: "no threat model, just a feature."
- 30-day test: **NO** — needs a credible security threat model + multi-tenant isolation that currently deadlocks.
- Venue: Workshop | Kill
- YOUR vote: **RED** (speculative; no threat model in evidence, multi-tenant isolation unproven).

## PART C — Top 3 NEW theses ranked

1. **NEW-T2** (cross-engine superlinear tool-injection penalty workload model) — most evidence already exists, cross-engine reproduced, anti-FlashInfer clean, 30-day feasible.
2. **NEW-T1** (heterogeneous-granularity cross-domain CoW abstraction) — highest novelty ceiling and the *only* angle the sibling committee itself flagged as genuinely-new, but the novel domains are entirely unbuilt.
3. **NEW-T3** (HW-loses-to-software crossover characterization) — most intellectually honest, data exists, but risks collapsing to an empty win-region under the anti-FlashInfer test.

**Which I'd defend as GREEN under hostile review:**

**NEW-T2, and only at workshop / MLSys-short tier.** Honest reasoning: it is the one candidate whose headline number is *real and not an oracle* (8.21× measured live through vLLM 0.6.6, reproduced superlinearly on SGLang), survives the anti-FlashInfer test cleanly, and needs no new system to be submission-grade. Its kill-shot — "cache invalidation is known" — is blunted by the superlinearity + cross-engine magnitude, which is not in any cited prior.

At **top-tier (ASPLOS/MLSys-full) I would defend NONE as GREEN.** NEW-T1 is the strongest *idea* but its novel domains are vapor (only KV CoW is built) — under this committee's own standard ("unbuilt = RED," cf. T4), I cannot vote GREEN on an unbuilt abstraction. NEW-T3 may prove HW forking is simply the wrong tool (700× / 6× losses to software), which is publishable but as a negative result, not a GREEN headline. The brutal through-line across all three repos: the *mechanisms* are largely subsumed by vAttention/ChunkAttention/CXLfork, so every survivable thesis here is a **characterization or workload-model**, not a system — and characterizations top out at workshop/short-paper GREEN until something novel is actually built (NEW-T1's non-KV domains being the highest-EV thing to build next).

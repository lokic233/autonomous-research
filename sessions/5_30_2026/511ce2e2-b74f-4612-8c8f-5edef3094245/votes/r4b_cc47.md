### B FINAL
- Vote: YELLOW
- Decision: YELLOW / workshop. The measurement work is solid — 3 engines, 5 contexts, 3 positions, 5 reps, fitted exponents 0.674/0.719/0.790 with R²≥0.97, position P25/P75 spread 2.2–2.5×, and a clean up-to-16.75× operational penalty that reproduces the prior 8.21× live number (8.39× here). That is a legitimate workload-characterization contribution. But the kill-test settled the headline: absolute recompute scales as N^1.29–1.31 (CI upper ≈1.41), which is firmly sub-quadratic and therefore not a novel scaling regime — it is ordinary bandwidth/compute-bound prefill, just triggered pathologically often by tool-call injection. A top-tier systems venue wants either a new scaling phenomenon OR a new mechanism OR a solution; B delivers none of the three. It delivers a careful quantification of a known-shape cost in a newly important workload. That's a workshop/industry-track paper, not MLSys main.
- Strongest remaining attack: "Your fitted exponents 0.67–0.79 on penalty(L) are an artifact of the denominator (cache-hit TTFT grows slowly with L), not a property of the pathology. The mechanism-level claim reduces to: recompute cost ∝ recomputed-token-count^~1.3, which is the standard prefill curve every serving paper already reports. Where is the contribution beyond 'tool calls cause prefix-cache invalidation, and prefill is expensive'?" The position-dependence result partially answers this (penalty ≈ f(recomputed tokens) is the right framing), but the paper still needs to argue why a workload model is needed when the underlying cost model is textbook.
- Venue you'd bet on: MLSys workshop (e.g., MLSys WORKS, ML for Systems, or an agentic-serving workshop) or an industry track. Possibly EuroMLSys. Not MLSys main, not OSDI/SOSP, not NSDI.
- One-line: An honest, well-measured workload characterization of a textbook sub-quadratic cost made operationally severe by agentic tool-calling — useful for practitioners, but capped at workshop because the scaling story is ordinary, not emergent.

### A — GPU VMM mapping-metadata ceiling (revised: vendor divergence)
- **Vote: YELLOW**
- **Strongest remaining attack:** The AMD result kills the architectural generality. What remains is "NVIDIA's driver has an internal ceiling at 520K page-mappings; AMD's doesn't." That's a compatibility footnote for VMM-based KV systems, not a research contribution. The predictive model `max_branches ≈ K/prefix_pages` is dimensional analysis (ceiling / denominator = quotient), not a model. Without access to driver source, you cannot explain WHY NVIDIA enforces this ceiling — you can only report that it exists. A measurement of one vendor's closed-source implementation behavior, even if precise (±1%), is a tech report, not a venue paper.
- **Evidence to flip GREEN:** (1) Root-cause the NVIDIA ceiling to a specific driver data structure (e.g., per-context VA descriptor table with fixed allocation), demonstrated via driver instrumentation or NVIDIA documentation confirming the design. (2) Show the ceiling has PERFORMANCE consequences before hard failure (e.g., mapping throughput degrades as you approach 520K, creating a soft wall). (3) Demonstrate that a real VMM-based serving system (not a microbenchmark) hits this ceiling under realistic multi-tenant load. Any ONE of these elevates "we measured a number" to "we explained a design constraint with system-level impact."
- **Venue:** Workshop paper at OSDI/ATC, or a section in a larger VMM-for-ML systems paper. Not standalone.

### B — Tool-call mid-prompt superlinear recompute pathology
- **Vote: YELLOW (closest to GREEN)**
- **Strongest remaining attack:** The superlinear penalty is trivially predicted from attention's quadratic complexity. If a tool response is injected at position P in context of length L, the suffix L−P must be recomputed, and attention on that suffix costs O((L−P)²). As L grows, (L−P)² grows superlinearly in L for any fixed relative injection point — this is high-school algebra on known attention cost, not a discovery. The cross-engine reproduction (vLLM and SGLang) confirms both engines lack a mitigation, but that's a feature gap report ("neither engine handles this"), not a workload model. Two context-length datapoints (4K, 32K) do not establish a scaling law.
- **Evidence to flip GREEN:** (1) Measure at 5+ context lengths (4K, 8K, 16K, 24K, 32K minimum) and fit an explicit scaling function with R² and confidence intervals — prove the exponent is WORSE than the quadratic-attention prediction (that would be genuinely surprising and point to additional system-level amplifiers like memory fragmentation or scheduler stalls). (2) Vary injection POSITION systematically (25%, 50%, 75% of context) and show the position-dependent model predicts penalty accurately — this is claimed in the sharpening ("model predicting penalty from injection POSITION") but I see no validation data in the evidence. (3) Measure on a third engine (e.g., TensorRT-LLM or a custom serving stack) to establish true generality beyond "two Python-based engines share the same gap."
- **Venue:** MLSys (short paper / findings track), or full paper IF the position-dependent model is validated and the exponent exceeds quadratic-attention prediction.

### C — When NOT to use hardware GPU CoW (negative result)
- **Vote: YELLOW**
- **Strongest remaining attack:** The thesis asks us to state whether the win-region is non-empty once FlashInfer is the baseline. Lab 3b answers this: contiguous-VA advantage over FlashInfer is only 5–22% throughput. Against that, HW CoW pays 700× fork latency and 6× capacity penalty vs software refcounting. For the win-region to be non-empty you need a workload that (a) demands unmodified contiguous-VA kernels (ruling out FlashInfer/PagedAttention), (b) has long shared prefixes, (c) tolerates 700× fork latency, and (d) cannot afford the 5–22% FlashInfer gap. Condition (a) AND (d) are contradictory in practice: if you can't tolerate a 5–22% throughput gap, you'd adopt FlashInfer rather than pay 700× latency to avoid it. **The win-region is likely empty**, but the thesis doesn't commit to saying so. A negative-result paper that hedges its own negative result is weaker than one that proves the boundary is closed.
- **Evidence to flip GREEN:** (1) Run the regime-map analysis to completion: sweep prefix length × branch count × decode length and plot the crossover surface where HW CoW breaks even with FlashInfer+software-refcount. If no crossover exists in the practical parameter space, state "the win-region is empty" and prove it — that's a definitive negative result worth publishing. (2) If a crossover DOES exist, characterize it precisely with workload parameters and show at least one real agentic trace (from the SWE-bench instances already measured in Metric 5) that falls in it. Either outcome (empty or precisely bounded) is publishable; the current hedged middle is not.
- **Venue:** EuroSys or ATC negative-result track, IF the win-region is definitively resolved (empty or bounded).

### FINAL

**GREEN today: none.**

All three are YELLOW. B is closest to GREEN but the "superlinear" claim currently lacks enough data points to distinguish genuine system-level amplification from trivially-predicted quadratic-attention cost on the recomputed suffix.

**The ONE experiment that would most efficiently flip a thesis to GREEN:** For Thesis B — measure recompute penalty at 5+ context lengths, fit an explicit power-law exponent, and compare it against the null hypothesis (quadratic attention cost alone). If the measured exponent exceeds the quadratic prediction with statistical significance, you have a genuine systems finding (additional amplifiers beyond attention cost). If it matches, you still have a useful measurement paper but need to reframe the contribution as "quantifying a known cost that practitioners underestimate," which is MLSys-short-paper territory rather than a flagship claim.

### A — GPU VMM mapping-metadata ceiling (K≈520K)
- **Vote: RED today.** Per the committee's own conditional it's "YELLOW if AMD doesn't reproduce" — AMD did NOT reproduce, so YELLOW is the *ceiling*, not the floor. I go RED because the corrected framing is not even clean yet.
- **Single strongest remaining attack:** The cross-vendor "divergence" is **confounded by page granularity and an artificial cap**, so it doesn't yet establish what it claims. The metric is *number of distinct VA mappings*. NVIDIA fails at 520K × 2MB ≈ up to **1TB of VA mapped**; AMD hit a *safety cap* at 4M × 4KB = **16GB of VA mapped** — far LESS total VA, and not a real ceiling. So "AMD tolerates more mappings" may just be "AMD has 512× smaller pages and we stopped early." At equal bytes-mapped the comparison might even invert. Layer on: (1) the universal "structural property of GPU VMM" framing — the only thing that made this paper-scale — **just collapsed**; (2) there is **no root-cause** for 520K (fixed table? PTE limit? tunable?), it's a magic number from a closed driver at one version (580.82.07) that can change next release. Round-1's "section not paper" kill wasn't answered — it got *stronger*.
- **What flips it to GREEN:** (a) Re-run AMD with the safety cap removed to **true failure**, AND control the comparison at *equal total-bytes-mapped* and *equal mapping-count* — show NVIDIA's per-context ceiling is genuinely and substantially lower, not a granularity artifact. (b) **Root-cause** the 520K (name the driver structure it exhausts) and show it's stable across ≥2 driver versions. Without (b) it's a benchmarked quirk, not a characterization. Even both → strong *section*, borderline standalone.
- **Venue I'd bet on:** None as a full paper. HotOS/workshop note, or a section inside the forkedkv systems paper.

### B — Superlinear cross-engine prefix-cache recompute pathology
- **Vote: YELLOW.** Most defensible of the three, still not GREEN.
- **Single strongest remaining attack:** The "superlinear exponent" is plausibly just **known O(n²) prefill attention cost showing through the ratio** — cached TTFT stays ~flat (new tokens only) while recompute scales with prefill, so the ratio rises mechanically. That's expected, not a discovered pathology. The genuinely-new parts of the sharpening — the **position-dependence model** ("predict penalty from injection POSITION") and an exponent *beyond* trivial quadratic — **are asserted but not in the evidence**. The brief only shows length-scaling (P1.3: vLLM 1.38×→5.41×, SGLang 1.61×→5.24×); zero position data is cited. So the evidenced part is the un-novel part, and the novel part is unevidenced. Cross-engine reproduction (vLLM+SGLang) and the live 8.21× are real and worth keeping — but measurement-only with the solution decoupled is a weak fit for MLSys main track.
- **What flips it to GREEN:** Ship the **position-dependence dataset** + a predictive model validated on held-out (position, context-len) points across BOTH engines, AND show the fitted exponent **exceeds the O(n²) prefill baseline** (i.e., refute "this is just attention scaling"). Then it's a real workload characterization, not a restated cache-invalidation fact.
- **Venue I'd bet on:** MLSys *workshop* or an IMC-style measurement track. Not MLSys main as measurement-only.

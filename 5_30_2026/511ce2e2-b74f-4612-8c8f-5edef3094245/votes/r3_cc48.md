### A*
- **Vote: YELLOW**
- **Strongest remaining reviewer attack:** The AMD side does not measure a ceiling — it hits *your own* 244GiB VA-reserve cap at 64M pages, so "absent on AMD" is really "not observed up to 123× NVIDIA's number." Worse, the comparison conflates granule size (2MiB×520K NVIDIA vs 4KB×64M AMD); mapping-metadata cost per entry is not normalized, so the 123× headline is partly a granularity artifact. And the NVIDIA root cause is *located* (fails at `cuMemSetAccess`, independent of `vm.max_map_count`) but not *explained*: is 520K a hardcoded table size, a metadata-memory function, or a tunable? "We found a magic number on one driver version (580.82.07)" is a section, not a top-venue paper.
- **Does the measured evidence resolve your round-2 objection? No (partially).** Round-2 T2 was "section not paper" because it was single-vendor. The second vendor was run and the divergence is real (NVIDIA hard-fails ±1% where AMD doesn't), which upgrades it from "characterization of one driver quirk" to "demonstrated portability cliff." But the two gaps that make it a paper — *mechanism* of the NVIDIA limit and an *actual* AMD ceiling (or multi-driver-version stability) — are still unmeasured. The vote can't go GREEN on a half-measured "absence."
- **Venue you'd bet on:** EuroSys/ATC *if* mechanism + AMD-ceiling added; today, a workshop or the measurement section of the C* paper.

### B
- **Vote: YELLOW**
- **Strongest remaining reviewer attack:** The core claim borders on definitional. Inserting tool output mid-prompt breaks the prefix hash chain → recompute, and recompute cost grows ~quadratically with contaminated-prefix length, so "superlinear penalty vs context length" is the *expected* shape of attention recompute, not a discovery. A "workload model" must be predictive/generative (parameterized by injection position, frequency, context length, model size, validated on held-out traces) — what's on the table is a two-engine sweep curve, i.e. a strong motivation figure. With the recovery half declared oracle/out-of-scope, it's "here is an expensive, known problem, no validated fix."
- **Does the measured evidence resolve your round-2 objection? No.** B is explicitly UNCHANGED — neither decisive experiment touched it. It sat at YELLOW in round 2 on exactly this evidence; nothing new can move it. Cross-engine confirmation (vLLM + SGLang both superlinear) is its real strength and keeps it off RED, but it is the same data.
- **Venue you'd bet on:** MLSys *as a section/short paper*; not a standalone full paper until the sweep becomes a validated predictive model or is paired with a non-oracle recovery.

### C*
- **Vote: GREEN**
- **Strongest remaining reviewer attack:** Two angles, both blunted by the data. (1) "Your HW arm is just badly built." Rebuttal: the 178µs/page is the *driver* remap cost (`cuMemMap`/`SetAccess`), and B8 measured only 7% as data copy / 3% removable — the floor is intrinsic to the VMM mechanism, not sloppy code. (2) "FlashInfer arm is analytic, not run end-to-end." Rebuttal: the margin is 41–152×; no plausible composition error closes that, and the brief's key move — *even a zero-cost KV mechanism cannot close it* — makes the result about kernel efficiency (paged vs contiguous-VA SDPA), not CoW cost, so it generalizes past the specific mechanism. The genuinely-open weakness is single-GPU/single-driver: AMD's 4KB-granule CoW avoids the 2MiB remap that kills NVIDIA here, so the *software-vs-HW* 1.06–2.20× edge may be NVIDIA-specific — but the *FlashInfer* gap is not, and that's the headline.
- **Does the measured evidence resolve your round-2 objection? Yes.** Round 2 demanded the end-to-end E-C experiment precisely because prior claims were micro-benchmark-only. It was run on a real trace, swept 12 cells, given HW its best fair shot (strongest HW rep, its hypothesised best corner: long prefix + rollback-heavy + contiguous-VA kernel), and the win-region came back empty (0/12), with rollbacks making HW *relatively worse* (1.06×→1.30×) exactly as the mechanism predicts. That is a clean, decision-relevant negative that kills the plausible hypothesis the committee itself cared about. Caveats (single layer, analytic FI) are honestly stated and don't dent a 41–152× margin because ratios are layer-count-invariant to first order.
- **Venue you'd bet on:** ATC/EuroSys negative-results / experience track.

### FINAL
- **GREEN: C\* only.** It is the one thesis with a *new* decisive experiment that returned clean and survives every hostile attack on the measured numbers (driver-intrinsic cost floor, ratio-invariant layer caveat, kernel-gap-not-mechanism generalization).
  - **A\* → not GREEN.** Single remaining experiment: characterize the *mechanism* of the NVIDIA 520K limit (is `cuMemSetAccess` failing on a fixed metadata table — tunable? memory-bound?) across ≥2 driver versions, **and** measure AMD's *actual* per-context ceiling (lift the 244GiB VA cap) so "absent on AMD" is a measured number, not an un-hit wall.
  - **B → not GREEN.** Needed change: convert the two-engine sweep into a validated, parameterized predictive workload model (injection position × frequency × context × model size, tested on held-out agent traces), or attach a non-oracle recovery. As-is the core claim is near-definitional and no new experiment was run.
- **Honesty check:** GREEN on C\* is on real measured evidence, not hope. The defense: I tried to break it and the data held — the empty win-region is across all 12 cells (not cherry-picked), HW given its best fair configuration still lost, the cost floor is a *measured* driver constant rather than implementation noise, and the one analytic arm has a margin so large (41–152×) that no reasonable error bar flips the conclusion. The result also *fails forensically* and points the field somewhere actionable ("don't build HW VMM CoW for agent KV with contiguous-VA kernels"). I deliberately did **not** extend GREEN to A* despite its fresh two-vendor data, because its AMD "absence" is an un-measured cap and its NVIDIA mechanism is unexplained — voting GREEN there would be agreeableness, not evidence.
